About Me

I am a theoretical physicist and Physics PhD candidate at UC Merced, working with Prof. Lin Tian on strongly coupled light–matter systems. At my core, I envision quantum systems and their behavior through effective Hamiltonian modeling and numerical methods—combining analytical techniques with sparse exact diagonalization and time-evolution solvers to uncover the spectrum, symmetries, and dynamics of closed and open quantum systems.

My doctoral research asks whether two coupled cavity–qubit systems can host a qubit encoded in hybrid light–matter (polariton) states through their static Hamiltonian, without continuous driving or engineered dissipation. I study the spectrum, symmetries, and closed- and open-system dynamics of this quantum Rabi dimer, and use electromagnetic simulations to assess a proposed superconducting-circuit implementation.

Alongside my dissertation work, I collaborate on Hamiltonian models of coupled magnon–photon systems at UC Merced, studied non-Hermitian spin-torque oscillator arrays during my M.S. at SJSU, and developed numerical routines for superconducting-qudit control and process tomography at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory. I also have extensive experience teaching physics—including serving as a Teaching Assistant and delivering multiple guest lectures in Computational Physics—and channel my core mathematical and numerical logic into building scientific and educational software tools as side projects.

I am seeking research opportunities in theoretical quantum physics, quantum information, and computational physics across academia, national laboratories, and industry.

Current Research

Quantum Rabi dimer: from coupled cavities to polariton qubits

Coupling two cavities changes their collective photon modes. In my doctoral work, I examine how the softening of one mode reshapes the low-energy spectrum into hybrid light–matter (polariton) states, and characterize the parameter regime in which an isolated even–odd polariton doublet forms a candidate qubit codespace.

The central question is how much of this structure can arise from a static Hamiltonian, without an external drive or engineered dissipation to stabilize it.

  • Spectrum and state structure. I combine normal-mode analysis with sparse exact diagonalization to identify low-energy doublets, resolve crossings using parity and site-exchange symmetry, and track states as the coupling changes.
  • Closed- and open-system dynamics. I calculate coherent state evolution, macroscopic entanglement, and photon distributions, and study how photon loss affects the proposed encoding. This separates unitary Hamiltonian evolution within the doublet from decoherence and leakage.
  • Circuit feasibility. I use COMSOL electromagnetic simulations and circuit models to extract mode frequencies, capacitances, impedances, and inductive participation. These calculations test which features of the theoretical model a proposed superconducting layout can realize and identify the remaining coupling constraints.

Status: Two manuscripts in preparation, covering the quantum Rabi dimer and its proposed superconducting-circuit realization. The circuit work is a modeling and feasibility study; experimental realization remains a future step.

The numerical work runs on SLURM clusters, with convergence checks and a version-controlled record of model conventions, simulation results, and open questions. An interactive simulation interface supports exploration of the model; unpublished code and results are available to discuss in interviews.

Technical Skills

  • Theoretical physics & Hamiltonian modeling: Effective Hamiltonians for light–matter, magnon–photon, and driven spin systems; normal-mode and Bogoliubov analysis; symmetry-resolved spectra; polariton-based qubit encodings; and closed- and open-system quantum dynamics.
  • Numerical methods: Sparse exact diagonalization, eigenstate tracking, unitary and master-equation time evolution, spectral and finite-difference PDE methods, convergence analysis, model fitting, and SLURM/HPC parameter sweeps.

  • Control and characterization: Pulse-level superconducting-qudit control, linear-inversion quantum process tomography, and Ramsey and T1/T2 analysis.
  • Quantum error correction: Stabilizer simulation, correlated-noise models, Monte Carlo benchmarking, Stim, and PyMatching.
  • Software: Python, NumPy, SciPy, QuTiP, Matplotlib, Flask, Git, Linux, pytest, and LaTeX; JavaScript and Google Cloud for web applications.

Computational Physics & Side Projects

Alongside my core research in Hamiltonian modeling, I build interactive simulations and side software projects to explore physical models and support teaching.

Quantum Rabi Dimer Simulation Interface

Interactive numerical workbench for exploring energy spectra, photon-exchange dynamics, and cavity-interaction regimes in the two-site Quantum Rabi model. Recomputes and visualizes eigenstates and time evolution in real time across coupling regimes (Python, NumPy, Flask, JavaScript; private repository while manuscripts are in preparation).

Sparse Diagonalization for Spin-Torque Oscillator Arrays

Sparse-matrix diagonalization workflow for large two-dimensional spin-torque oscillator arrays, supporting calculations of complex non-Hermitian spectra and oscillatory edge modes. View Code on GitHub

Physics Concept Analyzer Tool (PCAT)

Web application deployed on Google App Engine for physics courses at UC Merced that guides students through concept identification, model assumptions, and mathematical setup before starting a calculation (Python, Flask, Google Cloud, JavaScript).
