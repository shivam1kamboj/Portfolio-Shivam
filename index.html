About Me

I am a Physics PhD candidate at UC Merced, working with Prof. Lin Tian on strongly coupled light–matter systems. I develop analytical models and numerical simulations to understand how interactions shape quantum states, and what those states could offer for storing and controlling quantum information.

My doctoral research asks whether two coupled cavity–qubit systems can support a bosonic qubit through their static Hamiltonian, without continuous driving or engineered dissipation. I study the spectrum, symmetries, and dynamics of this quantum Rabi dimer, and use electromagnetic simulations to assess a proposed superconducting-circuit implementation.

At Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, I developed Python tools for superconducting-qudit control and quantum process tomography. Alongside my research, I build scientific software for quantum error correction and tools for physics teaching.

I am seeking research opportunities in quantum computing and computational physics across industry, national laboratories, and academia.

Current Research

Quantum Rabi dimer: from coupled cavities to bosonic qubits

Coupling two cavities changes their collective photon modes. In my doctoral work, I examine how the softening of one mode enables cat-like states in the low-energy spectrum, and characterize the parameter regime in which an isolated even–odd doublet forms a candidate bosonic-qubit codespace.

The central question is how much of this structure can arise from a static Hamiltonian, without an external drive or engineered dissipation to stabilize it.

  • Spectrum and state structure. I combine normal-mode analysis with sparse exact diagonalization to identify low-energy doublets, resolve crossings using parity and site-exchange symmetry, and track states as the coupling changes.
  • Dynamics and noise. I calculate state evolution, entanglement, and photon distributions, and study how photon loss affects the proposed encoding. This separates coherent evolution within the doublet from decoherence and leakage.
  • Circuit feasibility. I use COMSOL electromagnetic simulations and circuit models to extract mode frequencies, capacitances, impedances, and inductive participation. These calculations test which features of the theoretical model a proposed superconducting layout can realize and identify the remaining coupling constraints.

Status: Two manuscripts in preparation, covering the quantum Rabi dimer and its proposed superconducting-circuit realization. The circuit work is a modeling and feasibility study; experimental realization remains a future step.

The numerical work runs on SLURM clusters, with convergence checks and a version-controlled record of model conventions, simulation results, and open questions. An interactive simulation interface supports exploration of the model; unpublished code and results are available to discuss in interviews.

Key Projects

nonmarkov_qec: quantum error correction under correlated noise

Open-source Python package for comparing surface-code memory under temporally correlated dephasing and a statistically matched white-noise baseline.

  • Built the pipeline from stochastic noise generation and Stim error injection to PyMatching decoding, Monte Carlo sampling, and bootstrap uncertainty estimates.
  • For the tested distance-3 and distance-5 codes, the estimated crossing remained consistent within uncertainty across correlation times of 0.2–200 cycles. This result applies to the tested per-qubit-independent data-gate dephasing model with uncorrelated readout.
  • The package includes 74 tests, type checking, continuous integration, and automated releases. Planned extensions include correlated syndrome and two-qubit-gate errors.

Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) Protocol Simulator

  • Overview: A web-based simulation demonstrating the inherent security of quantum encryption through the BB84 Quantum Key Distribution protocol.
  • Tech Stack: Python, Flask, HTML/Jinja2, and Google Cirq for backend quantum circuit simulations.
  • Features: Simulates the key exchange between Alice and Bob, and includes a module to introduce an eavesdropper (Eve), visually demonstrating how measurement disturbs quantum states and reveals interception attempts.

View Repository on GitHub

Quantum Error Correction: Surface Code Simulation

  • Overview: A Python-based simulation of a distance-3 surface code, built to demonstrate foundational principles of quantum error correction.
  • Tech Stack: Python, NumPy.
  • Features: Initializes a grid of data qubits, introduces random bit-flip errors, measures stabilizers to generate error syndromes, and tracks error candidates via overlapping stabilizers to correct errors and restore the logical state.

View Code on GitHub
